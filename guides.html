The one idea behind SimpleMoney

Every transaction answers two separate questions: what was the money for? (a category, like Food) and where did it come from? (an account, like Cash or HDFC Bank). Most apps blur the two. SimpleMoney never does.

For example

You spend ₹500 on dinner from HDFC Bank and ₹200 on tea in cash. Food shows ₹700 for the month; HDFC Bank goes down by ₹500 and Cash by ₹200. When you later withdraw ₹5,000 from an ATM, that is a transfer from the bank to Cash. It moves money, so it is never counted as spending.

That is the whole model: three kinds of transaction (expense, income, transfer), any number of accounts, and categories that belong to no account.

Add your first account

Why it’s there

An account is anywhere money sits: cash in your wallet, a bank account, a card, a UPI wallet. Giving each one its real balance is what lets the app tell you how much you actually have.

For example

You keep cash at home, a salary account and a wallet app. Add all three with today’s balances and the total on Home matches what you really own.

How to

Closing an account later? Open it and archive it. It disappears from pickers and totals, but its old transactions stay readable.

Record an expense

Why it’s there

If logging a coffee takes a minute, nobody keeps doing it. Each step asks one question and moves on, so a normal expense takes a few seconds.

For example

You pay ₹190 for a filter coffee from your UPI wallet. Tap +, type 190, Food & Beverage, Cafe, UPI Wallet, save.

How to

Income works the same way: choose Add income, then pick a source and the account it was deposited to.

Tap any transaction later to edit or delete it. Deleting reverses its effect on the account balance.

Move money between accounts

Why it’s there

Withdrawing cash or topping up a wallet doesn’t make you any poorer. Recording it as a transfer keeps both balances right without inflating your spending.

For example

You take ₹5,000 out of an ATM. HDFC Bank drops by ₹5,000, Cash rises by ₹5,000, and “Money out” for the month doesn’t move.

How to

If the two accounts use different currencies, you’re also asked how much actually arrived, with the latest exchange rate filled in as a starting point.

Shape your categories

A sensible set of categories is ready on day one, but everyone’s life is different. You can rename, add, nest and re-icon them until the reports read the way you think.

For example

You want coffee separated from groceries. “Cafe” lives under Food & Beverage, so it still counts toward your food total while showing up on its own line.

Fix a balance that’s drifted

Why it’s there

Sometimes the app and your bank disagree and you don’t want to hunt for the missing entry. Adjust balance corrects the number directly, without inventing a fake transaction.

For example

Your wallet shows ₹1,340 in the app but there’s ₹1,300 in it. Set the balance to 1,300 and move on.

Set a monthly budget

Why it’s there

Knowing you spent ₹3,900 on food is trivia. Knowing you have ₹1,100 left for the next 22 days is a decision. Budgets are part of the app, not a paid extra.

For example

You give Food & Beverage ₹5,000 a month. Nine days in, the app says you’re spending fast and shows a safe daily amount to stay inside it.

How to

Transfers never count against a budget. Use the month selector at the top to look back, and the account selector to switch currency.

Loans, debts & subscriptions

Track a loan and its EMIs

Why it’s there

A loan is more than a monthly debit. You want to know how much principal you’ve cleared, what the interest is costing, and when it ends. The app handles fixed EMIs, interest-only loans and flexible repayments.

For example

You take a ₹4,00,000 car loan at 9.5% for five years. The app estimates the EMI at ₹8,400.74, reminds you before each due date, and shows the payoff curve shrinking.

How to

If the money landed in your bank (a personal or gold loan), you can add it to an account. For a home or car loan the lender pays the seller, so nothing is added. Late fees and interest adjustments can be entered when you record a payment, and are never added to your principal.

Keep track of money lent and borrowed

Why it’s there

Informal loans between friends and family are the ones that get forgotten. Tracking them per person means “how much does John owe me?” always has an answer.

For example

You lend Shafeek ₹4,000. Later he pays back ₹1,500; you tap Got paid and the balance with him drops to ₹2,500.

How to

“Choose from contacts” uses Android’s own picker, so the app only ever sees the one person you pick and needs no contacts permission. Switch off Update account balance if you just want a record without touching an account.

Stay ahead of subscriptions

Why it’s there

Small recurring charges are easy to forget and add up fast. One list, and a six-month forecast, makes them visible before they hit.

For example

The forecast shows October as your heaviest month because a quarterly gym plan renews alongside the monthly ones. You see it coming a month ahead.

Settings & extras

Use more than one currency

Why it’s there

If you work abroad, travel, or keep savings in another currency, you need both views: each currency on its own, and one combined picture.

For example

You have rupee accounts and a Saudi riyal account. The combined total is shown in rupees with a ≈ sign, and you can switch to riyals alone whenever you like.

How to

Rates are only downloaded once you actually have two currencies, and the request contains a currency code and nothing else. Combined charts use the latest saved rates, so treat them as estimates. Budgets and transactions always keep their original currency.

Back up to Google Drive

Why it’s there

Your data lives on your phone, which is great for privacy and bad if the phone is lost. Backup is the safety net, and it’s entirely optional.

For example

Your phone dies. On the new one you install SimpleMoney, sign in with the same Google account, and everything comes back.

How to

The backup goes to a hidden app-only folder in your own Google Drive. SimpleMoney has no server and never sees it. It’s a backup you restore, not live syncing between two phones in use at the same time.

Lock the app and hide amounts

Why it’s there

Your balances are personal. The lock uses your phone’s own fingerprint, face or PIN, so the app never sees or stores them.

For example

You hand your phone to someone to show a photo. SimpleMoney asks for your fingerprint, and amounts stay as dots until you tap the eye.

How to

Your phone needs a screen lock set up first. Authenticate to show amounts protects hidden balances, and Hide app preview covers the app in the recent-apps view.

Export, import or delete your data

Why it’s there

It’s your data, so you can take it out, bring it back, or erase it. There’s no lock-in.

For example

At tax time you export your transactions as a CSV and open it in Google Sheets to total your medical spending.

How to

Importing replaces what’s on this phone, and asks you to confirm first. Deleting from the phone leaves your Google Drive copy alone; deleting from Drive also turns sync off.

Change how it looks

Why it’s there

You open this app every day, so it should feel like yours and be easy on the eyes at night.

How to

Pay with UPI (optional)

Why it’s there

The annoying part of UPI is remembering to log the payment afterwards. This joins the two: scan, pay in Google Pay, and record the expense when you come back.

For example

At a shop you scan their QR inside SimpleMoney, pay in Google Pay, return, and add the category. Nothing to remember later.

How to

SimpleMoney never handles your bank or UPI credentials and doesn’t process the payment. The camera is used only for scanning. The first time, you may be asked for approximate location, which is used only to note the nearby locality on that expense; you can say no.

FAQ

Do I need to sign up or be online?

No. There is no account to create and the app works fully offline. The internet is only used for the optional Google Drive backup and, if you hold two or more currencies, for downloading public exchange rates.

Where is my data stored?

In a database on your phone. SimpleMoney has no server of its own. If you turn on backup, a copy goes to a hidden folder in your own Google Drive that only this app can read.

How do I move to a new phone?

If you use Google Drive backup, sign in with the same Google account on the new phone and your data is restored. Without Google, use Your data → Export all data, move the file across, and import it.

Can two phones share the same data?

Not at the same time. Backup is for restoring after a reinstall or on a new phone; changes made on two phones aren’t merged.

Why isn’t a transfer counted as spending?

Moving money from your bank to your wallet doesn’t change how much you have. Transfers stay out of income, spending and budgets so those numbers remain honest.

How should I track a credit card?

Add the card as its own account and record purchases against it. When you pay the bill, record a transfer from your bank account to the card.

Does SimpleMoney move real money?

No. It records and organises. With the optional UPI feature the payment happens in Google Pay, and SimpleMoney only saves the expense.

Is there an iPhone version?

Not at the moment. SimpleMoney is built for Android.

How do I delete everything?

Go to More → Settings → Your data. You can delete the data on your phone and, separately, the copy in Google Drive. Uninstalling the app also removes the data on the phone.
