The one idea behind SimpleMoney

Every transaction answers two separate questions: what was the money for? (a category, like Food) and where did it come from? (an account, like Cash or HDFC Bank). Most apps mix the two up. SimpleMoney keeps them apart.

For example

You spend ₹500 on dinner from HDFC Bank and ₹200 on tea in cash. Food shows ₹700 for the month. HDFC Bank goes down by ₹500 and Cash by ₹200. Later you take ₹5,000 out of an ATM. That is a transfer from the bank to Cash. It only moves money, so it is never counted as spending.

That is the whole idea: three kinds of transaction (expense, income and transfer), any number of accounts, and categories that belong to no account.

The four tabs

Home shows how your month is going. Transactions is your statement. Budgets shows your limits. More holds everything else: accounts, categories, debts, loans, recurring payments, import, the AI assistant and settings.

The first time you open the app

Why it’s there

A short welcome sets up the few things the app needs. Every step except the currency is optional, and you can change all of it later.

What you see

If you allow SMS, the app reads your bank messages, finds your accounts and fills in the balances it can. It then asks you to check each balance. Banks don’t always send the latest balance, so a quick check is worth it.

Everything works without signing in and without the internet. You can turn any of these on later in More → Settings.

Add your first account

Why it’s there

An account is anywhere money sits: cash in your wallet, a bank account, a credit card, a UPI wallet. Giving each one its real balance lets the app tell you how much you actually have.

For example

You keep cash at home, a salary account and a wallet app. Add all three with today’s balances, and the total matches what you really own.

How to

More than one currency

Each account has its own currency. You can keep a rupee account, a dollar account and a riyal account side by side, and each one shows its balance in its own currency. Choose the currency when you add the account.

Your main currency is used for combined totals, which are shown with a ≈ sign as an estimate. You can change it in More → Settings → Main currency. Exchange rates are only downloaded once you have accounts in two or more currencies. See Use more than one currency for the full story.

For a bank or card, you can add the last 2 to 4 digits of the account number. The app uses them to match bank SMS to the right account.

Closing an account later? Open it and archive it. It disappears from pickers and totals, but its old transactions stay readable. Archived accounts are under the ⋮ menu on the accounts page.

Loans, debts & recurring payments

Track a loan and its EMIs

Why it’s there

A loan is more than a monthly debit. You want to know how much principal you have cleared, what the interest is costing, and when it ends. The app handles fixed EMIs, interest-only loans and flexible repayments.

For example

You take a ₹4,00,000 car loan at 9.5% a year for five years. You answer a few questions, the app reminds you before each due date, and the payoff chart shows the loan shrinking.

Add a loan

Open More → Loans and tap +. The app asks one question at a time, in 8 short steps.

Use it every month

If the money landed in your bank (a personal or gold loan), you can add it to an account. For a home or car loan the lender pays the seller, so nothing is added. Late fees and interest adjustments are never added to your principal.

Keep track of money lent and borrowed

How to

“Choose from contacts” uses Android’s own picker, so the app only ever sees the one person you pick and needs no contacts permission. Switch off Update account balance if you just want a record without touching an account.

Automatic & smart tools

Add payments from bank SMS

Why it’s there

Typing in every payment is the part most people give up on. Your bank already texts you each one. With your permission, SimpleMoney reads those messages on your phone and adds the transactions for you.

For example

You pay ₹1,299 on a shopping app by UPI. Seconds later your bank sends an SMS. SimpleMoney adds a ₹1,299 expense to the right account and shows a notification asking what it was for.

How to turn it on

If a message comes from an account you haven’t added yet, the app creates it for you using the last digits in the message. You can rename it later.

Good to know

Only bank transaction messages are used. OTPs and personal messages are ignored. Messages are read on your phone and are never uploaded. You can turn it off any time in Settings, or in Android’s app permissions.

If a bank message was missed or read wrongly, open More → Help → Report SMS. You pick the message and it is sent privately to the developer to improve the reader. Nothing is sent unless you choose to report one.

Your phone may block SMS access for apps installed outside Google Play. If so, open the app’s settings, tap ⋮, choose Allow restricted settings, then allow SMS.

Import a bank or UPI statement

Why it’s there

If you didn’t use SMS, or you want to fill in old months, you can import a statement instead of typing each line.

For example

You download your last three months of Google Pay history and a bank statement. You pick both files, and each payment is added once, even if it appears in both.

How to

Before anything is added you get a review. You can see what will be added, which payments are already in SimpleMoney, and which are transfers between your own accounts. If a CSV or Excel file has unusual columns, you are asked to tell the app which column is the date, the amount and so on. It remembers that layout for next time.

Use Claude or ChatGPT with your money

Why it’s there

Sometimes it is easier to ask than to tap. You can connect Claude or ChatGPT and say “add these expenses”, “what did I spend on food?” or “find my subscriptions”. Nothing changes in your data until you approve it.

For example

You paste a loan statement into Claude. It works out the loan details and every payment, and sends them to SimpleMoney as a suggestion. You open the app, read it, and tap approve.

How to

When Claude or ChatGPT asks for a code, come back and tap Get pairing code. Then tap Start and choose 15 minutes, 30 minutes or 1 hour. The AI can only help while the session is on, and it ends by itself.

Anything it suggests waits in Suggestions until you approve or reject it. Suggestions you decide on are kept for 30 days, so you can look back.

The AI assistant is free for now. It is listed as a Pro feature in the Free versus Pro table.

Teach the app a bank SMS it can’t read

Why it’s there

The app already reads SMS from many banks. A small bank, a new message wording or a wallet may still be missed. Instead of waiting for an app update, you can ask the AI to write a reading rule for it. It uses only your missed messages, tests the rule first, and nothing is saved until you approve it.1

For example

Your bank sends “INR 2,340.50 has been withdrawn from your A/c ending 7788 at ATM MG Road”. The app doesn’t know that wording. After you approve one AI rule, every ATM withdrawal like it is read, with the right account and amount.

How to

Not happy with a suggestion? Tap Reject. Nothing changes. Approved and rejected suggestions are kept for 30 days under the Applied and Rejected tabs.

1 How an SMS rule works. A rule is a pattern for the shape of a message, not for one message. The AI keeps the fixed words, for example “has been withdrawn from your A/c ending”, and marks the parts that change, like the amount, account digits and place, as blanks to fill. It never puts one message’s amount or merchant into the pattern. Before suggesting, it runs the pattern on your real missed messages in the app’s own reader and checks that every one is read right. A rule is attached to the sender ID (like KERLBK) and says if money went out or came in. For this job the AI looks at the messages the app could not read.

Teach the app a new statement layout (PDF, CSV or Excel)

Why it’s there

Every bank lays out its statement differently. When the app doesn’t recognise a layout, it needs to know which column is the date, which is the amount and so on. You can pick the columns yourself, or let the AI work it out. The AI sees only the column names and up to 5 example rows, never the whole file.

For example

A small bank’s PDF has columns called “Value Dt”, “Particulars”, “Withdrawn”, “Received” and “Closing”. The app finds the date and description but can’t tell which columns hold money in and money out. Claude maps them in a few seconds.

How to

You can also fix the columns by hand on the same screen. Tap any column to change it. Either way, the layout is remembered, so the next statement with the same columns imports straight away.1

1 How the app knows a statement’s layout. It does not use the file name, the bank name or the file type. For a table, the app looks for the header row, which is the row of column titles, within the first 50 rows. It lowercases the titles, ignores extra spaces and keeps their order. That list is the layout’s identity. The next file whose column titles match, in the same order, uses the saved column choices. If the bank renames a column or changes the order, it counts as a new layout and you are asked again. PDFs from Google Pay, PhonePe and Paytm are different: the app recognises them from the text inside the PDF and reads them with built-in rules, so they never need this step.

Share a fix, or report one that reads wrong

Why it’s there

A rule that works for you probably works for everyone with the same bank. Sharing sends the format to other users so they don’t have to teach it again. If a fix that someone else taught reads your messages wrong, you can switch it off for yourself and report it.

Share a fix you just approved

Only formats and column names are shared. Your messages, amounts and data are never sent.1

Use and report shared fixes

Report a message the app gets wrong

A report is sent privately, and only the developer can see it. Nothing is sent unless you tap Send.

1 What is shared, and what is not. Sharing a fix sends the pattern only: the fixed words of the message, or the column titles of a statement, and nothing that came from your own transactions. Reporting a message is different. It sends the message you picked, with your note, in an encrypted package to the developer so the reader can be improved. Use it only for messages you are happy to share, and remove personal details from your note.

Pay with UPI (optional)

Why it’s there

Some banks send an SMS alert for every payment, but many send them only now and then, or not at all. Then a UPI payment never reaches SimpleMoney by itself. Paying from inside the app fixes that: it notes the payment when you scan, and reminds you later to record it, so nothing is forgotten.

For example

Your bank rarely sends SMS alerts. At a shop you scan their QR inside SimpleMoney and pay in Google Pay. A little later a notification asks Did you pay ₹250 to Fresh Mart? with a few likely categories. Tap one and the expense is saved. If you did not pay, dismiss it.

How to

SimpleMoney never handles your bank or UPI credentials and does not process the payment. The camera is used only for scanning. The first time, you may be asked for approximate location, which is used only to note the nearby area on that expense. You can say no.

Back up to Google Drive

Why it’s there

Your data lives on your phone, which is great for privacy and bad if the phone is lost. Backup is the safety net, and it’s entirely optional.

For example

Your phone dies. On the new one you install SimpleMoney, sign in with the same Google account, and everything comes back.

How to

The backup goes to a hidden app-only folder in your own Google Drive. SimpleMoney has no server and never sees it.

Lock your backups with a PIN

Your Drive backup is private to the app, but you can lock it with a PIN as well, so only you can open it. On the Sync page, turn on Encrypt Drive backups.

You then enter it once more to confirm, and tick that you have saved it. New backups are encrypted before they leave your phone. Older Drive copies keep their existing protection.

SimpleMoney never sees your PIN and cannot recover it. If you lose it, those encrypted backups stay locked.1 Data already on your phone stays available. You will need the PIN when you sign in again or restore on a new phone. You can turn encryption off later with your current PIN.

1 How the encryption works. Your PIN is turned into a 256-bit key on your phone, using a standard method called PBKDF2 with SHA-256. It runs 600,000 times with a random salt, which makes guessing a PIN slow. Each backup file is then locked with that key using AES-256-GCM, a widely used standard that also notices if a file has been changed. This happens before the upload, so Google Drive only ever holds scrambled data. The PIN itself is never saved or sent anywhere. The key is kept in your phone’s secure storage, so you are not asked for the PIN on every sync. On a new phone, the app asks for your PIN and builds the same key again. A longer mix of letters and numbers is harder to guess than a short one.

It is a backup you restore, not live syncing between two phones used at the same time.

FAQ

Do I need to sign up or be online?

No. There is no account to create and the app works fully offline. The internet is only used for the optional Google Drive backup, for downloading public exchange rates if you hold two or more currencies, and for the occasional ad in the free version.

Is it safe to let the app read my SMS?

Messages are read on your phone and are not uploaded. Only bank transaction messages are used, and OTPs and personal chats are ignored. The only time a message leaves your phone is if you choose to report it from Help → Report SMS. You can turn it off in Settings → Transactions from SMS.

Will I be bombarded with ads?

No. There are no banners, and nothing pops up while you open the app, add transactions or browse. The free version shows a short full-screen ad only after a bigger action, such as importing a statement or saving a loan. Ads are spaced a few minutes apart and limited each day. A few extras, like adding more than 7 budgets, ask first and let you choose to watch one. Pro removes them all.

Where is my data stored?

In a database on your phone. SimpleMoney has no server of its own. If you turn on backup, a copy goes to a hidden folder in your own Google Drive that only this app can read. You can lock that copy with a PIN.

How do I move to a new phone?

If you use Google Drive backup, sign in with the same Google account on the new phone and your data is restored. If you used a backup PIN, you will need it. Without Google, use Your data → Export all data, move the file across, and import it.

Can two phones share the same data?

Not at the same time. Backup is for restoring after a reinstall or on a new phone. Changes made on two phones are not merged.

Why isn’t a transfer counted as spending?

Moving money from your bank to your wallet doesn’t change how much you have. Transfers stay out of income, spending and budgets so those numbers remain honest.

How should I track a credit card?

Add the card as its own account (choose Credit card) and record purchases against it. When you pay the bill, record a transfer from your bank account to the card.

The app guessed the wrong category. What now?

Tap the transaction and change the category. You can also fix it from the “What was this for?” notification.

Does SimpleMoney move real money?

No. It records and organises. With the optional UPI feature the payment happens in Google Pay, and SimpleMoney only saves the expense.

Is there an iPhone version?

Not at the moment. SimpleMoney is built for Android.

How do I delete everything?

Go to More → Settings → Your data. You can delete the data on your phone and, separately, the copy in Google Drive. Uninstalling the app also removes the data on the phone.
